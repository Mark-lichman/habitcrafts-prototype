## 1. What makes something an ICP candidate here

This is the reusable part. These seven tests emerged from evaluating six segments, and they let
a new candidate be assessed without redoing the research. A segment that fails test 1 or 3 is
not a near-miss — it is structurally wrong for this product.

1. **The creator owns a knowledge corpus, not a programme.** A corpus — a book, a method, a
   syllabus, a curriculum — can be bound into lessons, checks and habits. A programme is
   already a sequence of prescribed actions and has nothing to bind. **This is the test that
   killed fitness**, and it is the one most easily mistaken for a competitive problem.
2. **The gap is already acknowledged by the buyer.** You are not there to convince anyone that
   people fail to practise what they learn. If the segment disputes the premise, the cost of
   education exceeds the value of the sale.
3. **They already compete on a number the bridge moves.** Rebooking rate, pass rate, client
   renewal, course completion. Practice Take Rate has to plug into something they already
   report, or it is a metric you are asking them to adopt on faith.
4. **The buyer is reachable in a morning.** An individual or a small organisation. Anything
   with procurement turns a six-week design partnership into two quarters.
5. **Daily practice is a plausible form of the work**, not an imposition on it. Mindfulness
   passes this perfectly; most knowledge work passes it weakly.
6. **Incumbents have not closed it.** Checked at the level the buyer experiences, not the level
   a press release claims — see the Preply case in §3.
7. **Independence from the other streams.** A candidate whose reply rate would correlate with a
   stream already running costs a slot and adds no information. This is an experimental test
   rather than a market one, and it is why an attractive intersection lost to a plainer segment
   in §5.

## 2. The five streams

Need = evidence the gap is felt and costly. Saturation = how much of it incumbents already
close. Both are four-step reads, deliberately coarse.

| Stream | Need | Saturation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language tutors | High · verified | Low at tutor level | Run first |
| Independent exec coaches | Very high · evidenced | Mid | Established practices only |
| In-house L&D | Very high · same evidence | Mid | 200–2,000 employees |
| Mindfulness teachers | High | Extreme consumer-side | Teacher-side only |
| Exam & certification prep | Very high · evidenced | Mid — SRS tools only | Replaced fitness |

**Language tutors.** The only stream with a verified gap and a warm first contact. Runs first
because it is the cheapest real answer available, not because the need is largest.

**Independent exec coaches.** The strongest commercial case on the list. Under 10–15% of
leadership training transfers to the job; 70% of programmes change no behaviour; **75% of
organisations see no improvement without follow-up and only 8% measure impact at all.** Those
last two together are the argument — this product is follow-up plus measurement, and the market
already knows follow-up is the missing ingredient.

**In-house L&D.** Same evidence, different buyer, different cycle. Split out as its own stream
rather than pooled with coaches, because pooling two economic actors under one label is the
mistake that made the E2 community experiment incoherent before it was split.

**Mindfulness teachers.** Semantically the best fit of any stream — daily practice *is* the
product — and commercially the hardest, because consumer mindfulness is closed. Only viable
narrowed to the teacher.

**Exam & certification prep.** Three structural advantages nothing else has: the exam date is a
built-in deadline so none of the motivation has to be designed; **pass rate is already a
published competitive metric**; and the corpus is native. JLPT is the wedge — N2 takes ~1,200
hours from zero, N1 passes at 28%, and the named reason candidates fail is poor planning.

## 3. Saturation is checked at the level the buyer experiences it

The Preply case is the reason this is stated as a rule.

Preply announced AI between-lesson practice in a press release. Their own help centre then says
Scenario Practice is *"currently available to a small group of English learners who have an
active subscription with a tutor"* — one language out of ninety-plus, behind a subscription, in
beta. A Japanese learner on the platform sees none of it.

**A feature that exists in a press release but not in a learner's week is not saturation.** It
is a validated category with an absent incumbent, which is a better position than an unvalidated
one. Check the help centre and the changelog, not the announcement.

The inverse also holds. Consumer mindfulness, consumer habit-tracking and the SRS ecosystem are
genuinely closed — the incumbents ship daily practice as the product, and the gap is shut at the
level the user experiences it.

## 4. Qualification filters

These are the operational half of the catalogue. Without them the streams are unworkable at
volume.

### Independent exec coaches — qualify on three or more, zero disqualifiers

Source from the ICF credential directory, because the credential tier does the filtering for
you and is publicly verifiable.

**Qualify:** ICF **PCC or MCC** (not ACC-only) · five-plus years visible · own domain and a
named practice · rates or engagement tiers published · named SMB or corporate clients ·
**runs a group or cohort programme** — weighted highest, because a cohort is a roster, which is
something to bind and something to measure · prior operating seniority before coaching.

**Disqualify:** ACC-only or no credential · "newly certified" or "transitioning into coaching" ·
coaching alongside a full-time job · a link-in-bio page instead of a domain · no named clients
and no published rates.

**Why the filter protects the experiment and not just the pipeline.** Coaches who are just
getting started reply *more* — under-booked and flattered by the attention — while having no
roster, no renewal pain and no budget. Leave them in and the best-replying sub-stream becomes
the least valuable one, which means a reply-rate signal does not merely get noisier, it points
the wrong way.

### In-house L&D — the headcount band is the qualification

**200–2,000 employees.** Above roughly 5,000, procurement turns weeks into quarters. Below 200,
there is no L&D function to sell to. Between them one person usually decides: Head of L&D, Head
of Talent Development, or People Development Lead — the last is frequently the real
decision-maker in a 200–800 person company.

### Language tutors and exam-prep coaches — audience band

**Target 1k–10k audience deliberately.** Booking rate falls roughly tenfold from sub-1k targets
to 50k+ in the closest measured analogue. Mid-tier is this product's market anyway, so the
constraint and the strategy agree.

## 5. Two rejections, recorded with reasons

**Fitness coaches — dropped on structure, not competition.** The need is the highest of any
segment considered; adherence is the whole problem. But a fitness coach has a *programme*, not a
knowledge corpus, so the binding step has nothing to act on — test 1. Coach platforms having
already shipped check-ins and habit tracking is the second reason, not the first. Only viable
re-entry: fitness *educators* who sell courses.

**Mindful leadership — a better market that lost on independence.** Mindfulness × management is
an established market with premium pricing and daily practice as the expected form of the work.
It is also an intersection of two streams already running, so its reply rate would correlate
with theirs rather than producing a fourth independent read — test 7. Kept as an *angle* to test
inside the coach and mindfulness streams rather than as its own slot. The same applies to
executive communication (language × management).

## 6. How an ICP is qualified as real

Twenty outreach messages a morning, four per stream. Fifty per stream in ten working days — or
forty once L&D was added, which is the cost of the fifth stream and the reason to run to day 17
rather than deciding at day 12.

**One rule makes this an experiment rather than a grind: hold the message constant and vary only
the stream.** Same structure, length, ask and channel; only the opening line and the
proof-of-familiarity sentence change. Improve the copy mid-run and the comparison is void.

**Reply rate is a weak absolute signal and a strong comparative one.** It measures the message
and the list at least as much as the need. So two harder signals are recorded on every reply,
neither of which good copywriting can produce:

- **Did they name the problem before you did?**
- **Did they chase you?** A second message without a nudge, a question about timing, an intro
  offered unprompted.

**Gates, on 40–60 sent per stream:**

- **Under 5%** — dead. At or below cold-email baseline on a hand-researched list.
- **5–15%** — ambiguous. Normal for competent outreach, so it proves the messaging works and
  nothing about the need. Decide on the two harder signals.
- **15%+** — live. Into the heavily-personalised band; worth a second 50 and a real ask.
- **25%+ and a third of repliers naming the problem unprompted** — candidate ICP, and the gate
  to narrowing the product onto it.

**Channel confound, stated because it is easy to miss:** L&D works on LinkedIn rather than cold
email. Keep email as the constant for cross-stream comparison and log LinkedIn pushes
separately, excluded from the reply-rate table, or a channel effect gets read as a market signal.

## 7. Unresolved

- **Whether the Preply gap holds beyond one learner.** The language stream rests on it. The
  help-centre evidence is solid on the beta being English-only and subscription-gated; the
  first-hand part is a single learner. If it is broadly rolled out and merely unsurfaced, the
  opening is much smaller.
- **Whether the L&D headcount band is findable at volume** on LinkedIn before four a morning is
  committed to it.
- **Where independent mindfulness teachers actually congregate**, and whether the largest
  meditation marketplace's teacher roster is a channel or a competitor.
- **Exam prep's saturation read is the shakiest here.** The SRS ecosystem is culturally dominant
  in language-exam prep specifically, and how much of the adherence gap it already closes for
  serious candidates has not been tested.
- **Twenty researched messages a morning is at the edge of possible** — roughly ninety minutes
  before anything else in the day. Dropping to twelve is better than diluting to templates,
  which collapses reply rates toward half a percent and burns the list.
