Paper and springs

A quiet paper studio where everything you touch springs back, and the things you keep turn gold.

This page is the system, not a tour of it. 1 · The concept

Everything you look at is paper: a warm parchment canvas, matte white cards with hairline borders, soft ink-blue text, one deep-blue hero surface per screen, grain instead of gloss. Everything you touch is a spring: it compresses under your finger, resists slightly, snaps back with a little overshoot. Calm and fun stop fighting because they live in different channels — calm is a material quality (colour, texture, spacing, stillness at rest), fun is a behavioural quality (physics, reward events). The environment never entertains; the objects never sit dead. Gold is not a paint colour; it is what long-kept things earn.

That split is not a mood, it is a test. Two rules fall out of it, and between them they settle arguments mechanically rather than by taste:

Paper stays quiet

The test: does the proposal decorate the environment? A gradient background, an ambient animation, a second accent in the chrome, a shadow under a resting card, a shimmer on a skeleton. If yes, it is vetoed — the room is not the show.

Nothing on this page loops except one leaf (§7). Nothing rests on a shadow except the floating layer (§4).

Objects must spring

The test: does the proposal leave an interaction inert? A tap with no press state, a completion with no spring, a toggle whose thumb teleports, a button that only changes colour. If yes, it is vetoed — the thing you touched has to answer.

The 450ms hold in §8 is the whole claim. Press one and let go early: it runs backwards at 2× and costs you nothing.

2 · Colour

The palette is closed. There is no new hex anywhere in this codebase; extensions happen in OKLCH, in tokens.css, or not at all. Raw --l-* (Daylight) and --d-* (Nightfall) ramps are declared once and never consumed directly — components read only the semantic --c-* layer below, which is remapped per theme in three places: :root, the prefers-color-scheme: dark block, and :root[data-theme="dark"]. The last two are identical and must be kept in sync.

Every hex and every ratio below is read from the live document and computed with the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula at paint time. Switch the theme in the control bar and this whole section re-measures itself — the two palettes can be compared without a reload. Translucent tokens are composited over their stated backdrop before measuring.

Surfaces and lines

Ink — blue as ink, never neutral grey

Brand blue — structure and action

In Nightfall the ramp collapses: the interactive steps all resolve to the one accessible brand tint and the structural steps to tinted surfaces. Toggle the theme and watch 500/600 converge.

Gold — the reward colour, rationed

Legal uses, and no others: the streak flame chip, milestone moments, gild marks, the calendar's milestone ticks, the nav's 3px selected pill, and the milestone card's single CTA. Never chrome fill, never a hairline, never text on light. gold-500 is 1.52:1 on the canvas — filled shapes only; text uses gold-700.

Sage — growth and success

Clay and amber — care, not alarm

Teal — retired

Kept only so illustration compositions have a legal fourth shape colour. Never a UI fill, never text, never a chart series. Chart series are brand-500, sage-600, gold-700, clay-600 — four maximum, direct-labelled.

Role tokens

These exist because the light and dark answers differ structurally rather than in value — a signal colour landing somewhere the raw ramp fails. They are the only things a component should reach for in that situation; the alternative is a dark-mode block in a component file, which this system does not allow.

3 · Typography

Nine sizes, real tracking, real line heights, rendered here at their true size. The floor is 13px, with no exceptions — caption is 13px, not the 11px the research table shows, and it differs from body-sm by weight and tracking rather than by size. Every value is a token; never hard-code a size. No webfont is loaded anywhere in this prototype, so the stacks name the intended faces first and then degrade through the best local humanist sans and transitional serif — which is why the display stack ends in Georgia and never in sans-serif.

Today's lesson

Where the display serif is allowed — and where it is not

Fraunces is a daily voice, not an event voice. Scoping it to milestones would mean 95% of sessions render 100% Figtree, and a neutral sans is exactly what the research flags as overused. Spend the personality budget where it is seen every day.

Allowed · --font-display

  • h1 page titles and the greeting
  • Streak numerals (.hero__numeral, .stat-tile__value)
  • Milestone headlines and the counting numeral
  • Empty-state headings
  • Article titles in the reading view
  • The Create Habit step questions
  • The celebration echo — display italic, sage-700
  • The wordmark in the nav drawer

WONK stays 0 everywhere except the milestone card, which is the one place it may go to 1.

Never · --font-ui

  • Body copy at every size
  • h2 and below — the serif is a display voice, not a heading font
  • Buttons and every control label
  • Navigation items and the FAB
  • Form fields, help text, placeholders
  • Chips, captions, metadata, the 13px meta line
  • Chat bubbles and the check-in feed

If the serif appears in chrome it is wrong. If it never appears in a session, that is also wrong.

Elevation — two layers, and only one of them casts

Paper is the canvas and every card: fill plus a 1px hairline and zero resting shadow. Floating is the nav bar, the FAB, modals and sheets, the hero card, and the desktop hover-lift — those are the only things in the system that cast. --shadow-xs and --shadow-sm do not exist and must not be re-added: if you want a shadow on resting content you want a tone step (--c-canvas-sunken) or a hairline. Because lift is rare, lift means something.

Paper — these rest, and cast nothing

.card surface + hairline + radius 16 + padding 16. No shadow, at any breakpoint, in either theme.

.card--sunken the tone step that replaces a shadow.

.card--tinted surface-tinted + brand-200 hairline.

.card--interactive hover it with a real pointer: −1px and --shadow-md over 120ms. Pointer-only, and the one shadow a card can earn.

Floating — the complete list of things that cast

--shadow-md desktop hover-lift only.

--shadow-lg the bottom nav bar, the FAB, modals and sheets, the skip link, the control bar.

--shadow-hero the deep-blue hero card, and nothing else.

Shadow colour is rgba(22,35,47,α) — the ink hue, never neutral black. In Nightfall all three tokens resolve to none: dark carries elevation by tone and border only. Switch the theme control to Dark and the three tiles above go flat while the paper row above them is unchanged — that is the model working, not a bug.

5 · Components

BEM-ish: block .card, element .card__title, modifier .card--hero, state .is-complete. States are always .is-*. Behaviour keys off data-* attributes and never off class names, so you can restyle any of this without touching js/prototype.js.

Buttons

Press is scale 0.98 over 100ms --ease-standard; release rides --ease-spring-snap at 180ms. Every button is at least 48px tall. .btn--gold is reserved for the milestone card's single CTA and appears nowhere else. .btn--danger is clay, never red-alarm styling.

6 · Icons

One family, one style: hand-authored, outlined, fill-0, rounded terminals, 1.7px stroke, 24 × 24 grid, currentColor. No Material Symbols file, no Lucide, no raster, no emoji-as-UI — with zero dependencies allowed, the only way to have one family is to draw it. Icons inherit --c-ink-muted from .icon at rest and brand-600 from a selected parent, with no per-icon CSS. Sizes are .icon 24 · --sm 20 · --lg 32.

41 symbols. The sprite block is canonical and byte-identical on all eleven files. If you add one, add it to the block and paste the whole block to every page — never fork one page's copy. This is a rule with history: two parallel builds each grew their own sprite, one redrawn in a solid family, and the drift was invisible until the files were diffed. The stroke attributes live on an inner <g>, never on the <symbol> — base.css carries a global svg { fill: currentColor } and a document rule beats a presentation attribute on the cloned symbol, so fill="none" on the symbol makes every icon fill solid.

7 · Illustration

The two-colour rule: brand-300 shapes with brand-700 linework, plus at most one accent element in sage or gold. If an illustration cannot be drawn in two colours, it does not belong. Vocabulary is abstract-botanical and geometric — leaves, stems, threads, stones, craft objects. Explicitly not: 3D renders, gradient blobs, stock characters, Corporate-Memphis people.

The rule is enforced in CSS rather than retyped as inline fills on every path, which is why every drawing below re-tones itself in Nightfall for free, with no second artwork and no dark-mode SVG — switch the theme control and watch. That matters because --c-illus-line has to invert in dark: in Nightfall brand-700 becomes the hero blue, so a stem drawn in it would vanish into the canvas. There used to be a second way to draw one (.empty-state__art, with the colours hard-coded inline); it could not re-tone, and it is gone. .illus is the only way.

Home · day complete. The one accent is .illus__accent--gold, because this is the reward state. Only the upper leaf and bud sit inside .illus__drift — the stone stays planted.

Home · no habits. Sage accent, because nothing is earned yet — gold belongs to the reward states.

The parts

.illus — the root. 132px, viewBox="0 0 120 120", role="img" plus an aria-label.

.illus__shape — brand-300 fill with linework stroke. The object.

.illus__line — linework only, no fill. Stems, threads, rules.

.illus__ground — the stone it rests on. Flat brand-300 at 55%, no stroke; the one place a wash is allowed without linework, because it reads as a shadow rather than an object.

.illus__accent / --gold — one per illustration, maximum. Filled, never a hairline.

.illus__drift — 4s, one axis, ±4px, alternate, infinite. The only looping animation in the product, and deliberately under the WCAG 2.2.2 five-second threshold. Wrap only the growing thing; under reduced motion it stops dead rather than fast, or the leaf parks 4px off its drawn position.

Auth's poster and the onboarding slide art are deliberately not .illus: they sit on the deep-blue surface, so they are drawn in --c-hero-ink / --c-hero-ink-muted, where .illus__shape's brand-300 would be near-invisible. They still consume semantic tokens and still re-tone. Do not "fix" them onto these classes.

8 · Motion

Six named moments. Every one of them is replayable below, and every one is shown beside its reduced-motion variant, because "reduced" means reduced, not none: what goes is transform-based motion — scale, translate, spin, the actual vestibular triggers — and what stays is colour and opacity, which WCAG 2.3.3 explicitly says are not motion animation. Durations shorten to --dur-micro; they are never merely sped up, because movement at 20× speed is still movement.

How the comparison works. The reduced path is a root-level state — [data-motion="reduce"] on <html>, which is what HC.reducedMotion() and every CSS branch read. It cannot be scoped to half a page without reimplementing the architecture, so each replay flips that attribute for the length of the run and restores it afterwards, and Replay both plays the two panes in sequence. Nothing below is a mock-up of a reduced variant: both panes run the same real code down the two real branches.

One honest consequence: if your OS already asks for reduced motion, the full-motion pane also plays reduced. The @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) block in base.css carries !important and the page cannot — and must not — defeat it. Use the control bar's Motion toggle to force the reduced path the other way.

9 · The veto list

Verbatim from docs/design-direction.md §6. It is not a style opinion; it is the enforcement half of §1's two rules. If any of these appear, remove them.

  • Glassmorphism anywhere except (optionally) the floating mobile nav — tinted, single layer, solid fallback. No frosted cards, no glass modals.
  • Purple/violet anything; mesh or rainbow gradients; animated gradient backgrounds.
  • Neumorphism; inner-shadow "pressed plastic" controls.
  • Drop shadows on resting content (4.3).
  • Confetti on ordinary completions; any celebration that loops or repeats per session.
  • Scroll-triggered reveals, parallax, scroll-jacking.
  • Streak-guilt UI: broken-chain icons, red missed days, "Don't lose your streak!" copy. Streak language follows the brief's Smashing rule — repair, grace, and "you showed up for 42 days," never "you lost."
  • "AI sparkle" iconography, glow effects on light surfaces, emoji as interface elements.
  • Any new hex value not in §4. The palette is closed. Extensions happen in OKLCH, in the token file, or not at all.

Three further laws that are easy to break by accident, and are checked the same way: teal is retired from the UI entirely; linear easing is banned except on progress bars (the check-in hold fill and the decay bar are the sanctioned exceptions); and 13px minimum text, no exceptions — including the scaffolding on this page.
