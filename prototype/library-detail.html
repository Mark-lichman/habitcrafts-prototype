Make it a habit

Week 5 · Making it easy · Lesson 4 of 5 · 4 min read

Celebrate in the first three seconds

The repetition is not what makes a habit stick. The feeling is — and the feeling has a very short window.

You already know the part where you do the thing. Two push-ups, one page, three breaths. That part is easy once it is small enough. The part almost everyone skips is what happens immediately afterwards, and that is the part your brain is actually keeping score of.

Why the window is short

A behaviour gets wired in by the emotion attached to it, not by how long you have been doing it. That emotion has to arrive while the behaviour is still happening or just as it ends. Three seconds later and you have moved on; your brain has nothing to attach.

This is why people can floss for a month and still not have a flossing habit. They did the behaviour thirty times and felt nothing thirty times. Repetition without feeling is just admin.

The habit is not what you did. It's how you felt about having done it.

What counts as a celebration

Anything that gives you a genuine flicker of "good". It does not have to be visible to anyone else and it should take no time at all. People who have made this work use things like:

  • Saying a word out loud — yes, done, good.
  • A fist pump, a nod, a small physical gesture.
  • Picturing someone specific being pleased with you.
  • One deliberate breath, taken as a full stop.

What does not count: a reward you have to go and get, anything that takes more than a few seconds, and anything you have to remember to do later. If it needs a plan, it will not survive a bad week.

Finding one that is yours

Borrowed celebrations feel like a script. Yours will feel slightly embarrassing, which is the sign it is real. Try three or four over the next few days and notice which one produces an actual flicker rather than a performance of one.

When you find it, write it down in the habit itself — HabitCrafts asks "How will you celebrate?" for exactly this reason, and it says your own words back to you every time you check in.

When it feels silly

It will, for about a week. That is not a reason to stop; the self-consciousness fades much faster than the wiring does. If it helps, do it where nobody can see you. The habit does not care whether there was an audience.

And on the day you miss: nothing was lost. You showed up for forty-two days. Tomorrow the same three seconds are still available.

Make it a habit

Reading it is not the habit. Give it a prompt, keep it small, and decide now how you'll celebrate — it takes about a minute.
